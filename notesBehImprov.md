## Course: Expanding Your Communication Skill Set
### Know your audience 
- Research
    - Demographics, Backgrounds, Communication Preferences
    - Prior knowledge to topic
    - Category (executies, technicians, end-users, experts, etc.)
- The why/purpose
- Listen to your audience
    - Provide opportunities, Q&A sessions
- Adjust as needed
    - Focus on reactions
    - Tone, non-bias, preferred communication styles
    - Reflect on past expeiences

### Choosing the right message format
- Use informal formats when establishing a personal connection
    - Ex. instant or text messages, often quick or time sensitive
    - Informal oral meetups (watercooler), chance meetup, face2face
- Use semi-formal communication formats based on organizational roles
    - Ex. written format or memo, functional/operational
    - Ex. email is more popular
        - Email ettiquette: subject line, professional tone, spell and grammer check, precise
    - Ex. team meetings, in-person or online or calls, prepare, respect time
- Back up all oral meetings with written communication when possible, key takeaways
- Use formal communication formats to communicate precise and consistent messages
    - Exchange official information between different levels
    - Ex. written report, clear objective and structure
    - Ex. one-on-one meetings, feedback, address concerns, discussions (positive and negative)
    - Ex. presentations, large audiences, proposals, training; visual aids, body language

### Navigating different communication styles
Most focus on what, not how <br>
Building a flexible communication styles is essential; usually fixed <br>
Match to your audience <br>
Four main styles of communication <br>
- Social, personal communcation
    - Enhances collaboration, open, inclusive, listening, ask questions, empathy & moral
    - Use this style when working closely with others and resolving workplace disputes
- Analytical, evaluative communication
    - Problem-solving, precision, data/metrics, observation over speaking, evidence over opinions
    - Use this style when need to backup decisions with metrics, id patterns/trends/root causes
    - Direct and concise conversations to answer hard questions
- Function, task-oriented communication
    - Execution, getting things done, focus minor/major details, implement plan/strategy, speaking over listening, processes over people
    - Use this style when incorporating numerous details w/o compromising delivery, finish in a timeline and budget, set/execute/maintain tasks/plans/deliverables
- Intuitive, expressive communication
    - Inspiration, visionary, ideas over data, 'big picture'
    - Use this style to brainstorm possiblities
    - Helps convery 'the why' rather than 'the how'

### Communicating well in difficult conversations
Situations where: you need to speak up; challenging and difficult <br>
Uses communication strategies to handle difficult conversations <br>
- First strategy: keep listening
    - Fully present in conversation; avoid distractions, don't interrupt, concentrate on what's beings said, Q&As, summerize/paraphrase
    - Body language: eye contact, relaxed posture, uncross arms
    - Listen attentively
    - Connection = trust, sense of safety
- Second strategy: acknowledge emotions
    - Yours and the others
    - Acknowledge others by asking open-ended questions, ie. "Did something I say upset you?"
    - Observe others body language, understand why & when others are upset
    - Demonstrate empathy
    - Acknowledge the conversation is difficult
    - Try to view from other's perspective
    - Give others a chance to speak before reaching a conclusion
- Third strategy: state the disagreement neutrally
    - 'You always' will cause offense
    - Neutral, impersonal language
    - Word choice + tone > escalate or de-escalate
    - Fact check before, anticipate questions and answers, don't engage in negative behavior (angry comments/insults), focus on issues
- Fourth strategy: be ready to negotiate
    - Opportunity to direct to potential solutions, overcome issues
    - Ask manager for help if needed; how to deal with difficult situation or find mutually agreeable solutions
    - Negotiate with empathy
        - When delivering difficult news, "I'm sorry about this, but..."
    - Build alliance with counterpart
